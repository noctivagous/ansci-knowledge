The correspondence web, in chords and bundles

The schemes held in ansci-knowledge connect the five phases （五行） to seasons, flavours, organs, grains, animals, the creature classes of the Yueling, and — through the trigrams — to the sixty-four hexagrams. Drawn as chords and edge bundles, the same assertions show their shape at three distances: the flows between groups, one source's table at a time, and every individual value tied to its phase. At the centre of each circular diagram a taijitu gauge reads the yin–yang proportion of whatever is selected — for trigrams and hexagrams the proportion is exact, counted from their lines (yang lines over 3, resp. 6); for the phases the values are schematic — Fire at peak yang (~90%), Water at peak yin (~10% yang), Wood rising yang, Metal rising yin, Earth at balance (~50%) — and are labelled schematic wherever they appear. At rest the gauge shows the balance of the whole displayed system.

1 · Flows among phases, flavours, seasons and creature classes

The five phases, the five flavours, the five seasons and the five creature classes （五蟲） — and, with the 洛書·八卦 layer on, the nine Luoshu positions and the eight trigrams join the same circle, so a position's ribbons to its trigram and its phase, and a trigram's ribbon to its phase, are drawn in the same view as the cycles. A ribbon runs from a node to what it produces, governs or is assigned to — the generating cycle （相生） and controlling cycle （相剋） among the phases themselves; the Hongfan's flavour production (each phase's process makes a flavour); Suwen ch. 4's phase–season assignments; the Yueling's season–class assignments; and the classes' phase membership as the Yueling commentary tradition states it. Ribbon width counts the kinds of link between two nodes. Turn link kinds off to read one system at a time. Click a ribbon or a node and the panel lists the assertions it aggregates — scheme, tier and source for each one; nothing in the diagram is only a count.

2 · The correspondence tables, one source at a time

Each source states its own table, and the tables do not all agree. All nine correspondence schemes of the repository are layers here — the two partial ones （淮南子·精神， a single passage; and the 月令's own attested cells) are marked partial, their missing cells left empty rather than completed from another source. The sources are layers: switch one off and every chord re-forms from the sources that remain. With several layers on, a chord's width counts the sources asserting that family for that phase, and a chord with a dark outline marks a cell where the sources disagree — the Shize animal row is transposed against Suwen ch. 4 (spring takes the goat, summer the chicken), and its grain row differs in three cells. Clicking any chord lists the exact assertions inside it, so a precise question — what does the Shize say about spring's animal? — is answered by the rows themselves, disagreements flagged and left unresolved.

3 · Every value, bundled to its phase

Hierarchical edge bundling draws each leaf on a circle, grouped by family, and routes its link through the hierarchy — links that share a path overlap into a bundle, so the weight of a whole family reads at a glance. Four link sets share the circle: the Suwen ch. 4 values tied to their phases; the Yueling creature classes with their members (attested members, and members the class criterion predicts — frog and spider have no assertion in the scheme and are absent, not filled in); and the eight trigrams tied to their phases in the Later Heaven arrangement, with each trigram also joined to its opposite in the King Wen pairing. Two trigrams, one above the other, compose a hexagram: 8 × 8 = 64, by composition rather than storage. Every edge is one assertion: click an edge or a name to read it in the panel. Ungroup separates the bundles into their individual edges; isolate keeps only the selected name's edges, drawn separately; zoom to selection spreads a selection until its edges are individually clickable. The Luoshu web sets the nine positions in the hierarchy with their edges to the trigrams placed on them and to their phases — the centre position has no trigram, and draws no such edge. The Hexagrams view changes the geometry itself: rings by derivation, phases at the core, the eight trigrams in the next ring, and the sixty-four hexagrams on the rim, each inside its upper trigram's sector — sector membership is the upper trigram, a short radial tie confirms it, and one edge runs to the lower trigram. Composed shows the sixty-four as single six-line units; Decomposed splits each glyph into its two stacked trigrams and lights both parent ties, folding the sixty-four back into the eight.

4 · How the hexagrams are composed

Eight nodes only: the trigrams. A ribbon runs from an upper trigram to a lower trigram, and there is exactly one hexagram for every ordered pair — the ribbon is that hexagram, numbered and named in the King Wen sequence. Ribbons that loop back to their own node are the eight pure hexagrams, one trigram doubled. Click a ribbon to see the hexagram it is, drawn as its six lines; click a trigram for the sixteen hexagrams it takes part in — eight as upper, eight as lower. The sixty-four are generated here by the 8 × 8 composition, not listed as an inventory.

The Luoshu 洛書 and Hetu 河圖 are held in the same repository as diagram-schemes — arrangements of numbers and directions in their own right; the number rows in the tables above （角 8, 徵 7, 宮 5, 商 9, 羽 6) are the phases' numbers as Suwen ch. 4 and the Shize state them, a separate scheme from the Luoshu's nine positions.
